Our AI platform is live and the product teams still go around it

Product teams skip a shared AI platform for three reasons. Find yours before you reach for a mandate.

Platform here means a shared AI service one team builds for others, such as an LLM gateway that routes every team's calls to AI models. Internal customers are the product or delivery teams expected to build on it, in your company, a parent company abroad or a client account.

The challenge

What is happening. Five months ago, your team at a global bank's capability centre in Bengaluru launched an LLM gateway that masks customer data, logs every call and tracks spend. Leadership funded it to stop 14 product teams building their own. Eleven have onboarded, but only two send production traffic, and one is your own demo assistant.

Why it hurts. Your dashboard says "11 teams onboarded", so nobody is asking why the platform sits unused. They will at the budget review in 8 weeks. At least 3 teams call models through their own cloud AI subscriptions, so customer data reaches models unmasked and unlogged, which in a bank is an open control issue. They say your gateway is slower and gets new models 6 weeks late. You have no revenue line and cannot order anyone to switch.

What is really going on

Your competitor is the direct API call or the chatbot people already use. In Microsoft's 2024 survey, 78% of AI users brought their own AI tools to work, and 72% in India [1][2], so the habit is there before your platform arrives. Teams show three kinds of non-adoption.

01

They are not sure what is allowed

They say"Nobody told us which tools we can use with customer data."

What is really happening
Without a stated rule, each team keeps what it started with, because nobody wants to guess wrong with customer data.
How to spot it
Ask three team leads which route customer data must take to a model; you get three answers, or none.
What to do
Turn your company's or parent's existing AI policy into a one-page reading for product teams. Start now, because sign-off in a regulated firm takes weeks.
02

The cost is theirs and the benefit is the company's

They say"Moving to your gateway means rework and a queue, and our roadmap gets nothing from it."

What is really happening
Masking, logging, cost control and reuse help the company. Migration work, added delay and less control fall on a product team judged only on its own launches.
How to spot it
The lead agrees in principle but cannot name a date to move.
What to do
Tie the move to something their launch needs, such as audit logs a client or regulator demands, or pay the switching cost.
03

They fear being stuck on an old model

They say"We tried it, but the model we need is not on your gateway yet."

What is really happening
Product teams move to newer models as they appear, so a gateway that adds them weeks late looks like a brake.
How to spot it
Teams left within weeks of a model release, and their requests name a model, not a feature.
What to do
A regulated firm reviews each new model for third-party and model risk, which you do not own, so promise when the review starts, not when the model arrives. Ask the team back when it clears. No published survey we found asks product teams why they leave a platform, so confirm this reason with them.
Stop asking

How do we get more teams onboarded?

Start asking

What did this team call last week instead of us, and why?

The mistake most teams make

Asking leadership to mandate the platform for every AI project.

More than a third of platform teams in a December 2025 community survey still rely on mandates [3]. Teams then take API keys and file exceptions while live traffic stays on direct routes, so your numbers rise and you stop hearing why. In a bank, exceptions carry expiry dates that often get extended. Ask leadership instead for a rule on which route sensitive data must take, and win the rest of the traffic by being the easier route.

The options you have

Most teams combine two or three, matched to the non-adoption they found.

Stop building the full platform and serve one team's launch

Pause everything beyond the gateway, such as an agent kit or prompt library. Build only what one product team's launch next quarter needs, with one of your engineers inside that team until launch.

Best when
No team runs production traffic through the platform yet, and one has a deadline and a problem you can solve.
What it costs you
Pausing funded work needs a formal re-plan with your sponsor or steering committee, and other teams wait a quarter.
Watch out
It fails if that team's needs are unusual, so choose a problem at least three other teams share.

Make the governed route the fastest route

Offer self-serve access and starter templates for common jobs, and review the platform's controls once so each use case needs a short attestation instead of a full risk assessment.

Best when
Teams would use the platform, but steps, approvals or delay stop them.
What it costs you
Your roadmap shifts from features to onboarding, documentation and model upkeep, which seldom impress a steering committee.
Watch out
It fails if the gateway adds delay users can feel, which easy onboarding cannot make up for.

Close the direct route for sensitive data, then let teams opt in

With your security and risk heads, set one rule that customer and confidential data reaches a model only through a logged, masked route. Inside that rule, let teams choose, offering the platform as the easiest compliant route.

Best when
Regulation or client contracts already demand the data rule, and your platform is as easy as the alternatives.
What it costs you
Security spends its authority on the rule, and teams with non-sensitive data stay outside it.
Watch out
Naming your platform as the only route while it lags on models invites exceptions, so allow approved vendor tools that meet the logging rule.

A capability centre usually follows its parent bank's model risk rules and group policy, so check those first. Where the platform serves the group's RBI-regulated entity, RBI's draft guidance of June 2026, not final as of September 2026, would bar that entity from using any model not in its inventory [4][5]. For Indian personal data, the DPDP Rules require logs kept for at least a year from May 2027, though a proposal to bring that forward was floated in early 2026 [6][7]. One logged route is easier to show an auditor than fourteen.

Pay the switching cost from the centre

Your engineers migrate the first teams, and the platform pays their model usage for a set period. Each team gets a monthly statement of what its usage cost but pays nothing, known as showback.

Best when
Team leads agree in principle but have no roadmap time to move.
What it costs you
Budgets are set yearly, and a capability centre's costs go to its parent, so the money comes from your run budget or the sponsor's. Agree it and a spending cap with the sponsor and finance first.
Watch out
A cost statement nobody pays is easy to ignore, so teams may leave when billing starts; announce its date and rate on the first day.

PM tools to put in place

A list of internal customers

Each team building with AI gets a row with its named lead, the data it handles, the next model it needs and its kind of non-adoption. One column records what it called last week: the platform, a direct API or a chatbot. Update it after every conversation.

A one-page reading of the AI policy

It says which models and tools suit which data, which route customer data must take, and how to request a new model. Security, model risk and compliance sign it off, and the platform's onboarding page links to it.

A published service promise

Report four targets monthly: access within one working day, each requested model's review started within a set number of days, the time the gateway adds to each call, and uptime. A public queue shows where each requested model sits. Publish misses as well as hits, because teams count them anyway.

Your first three steps

  1. 01

    Ask every team lead building with AI, direct callers first, what they called last week, with what data, and why. Mark each team's kind of non-adoption; the mix tells you whether to fix the rules, the funding or the model delay. Raise direct routes carrying customer data with operational risk or security as open control issues, so each gets an owner and a fix date before an audit finds them.

  2. 02

    Onboard yourself as a new team would, timing every step to your first successful call and the delay the gateway adds. You will know which steps to cut before a team tells you.

  3. 03

    Book 30 minutes with your sponsor to replace "teams onboarded" with "teams in production" as the platform's reported number. Changed now, it is a decision; changed at the budget review, it looks like an excuse.

What to measure, and how

Check these every week, in this order.

01

How many teams run live features through the platform?

A team in production depends on you and vouches for you to other teams.

How to measure it
Count teams whose live feature sent real traffic through production keys in the last 7 days, excluding your own demos and health checks. Track teams that stopped separately.
Good sign
The count rises every few weeks and no team stops.
Warning sign
The count is flat while onboarding rises, or a live team's traffic falls.
What to do: Call that team's lead within the week and ask what it calls now, and why.
02

How much of the company's AI spend goes around the platform?

Spend outside the platform and outside approved logged tools is traffic nobody masks or logs.

How to measure it
Each week, compare the AI lines in your cloud billing export, including teams' own AI subscriptions, and model providers' usage dashboards with the spend the platform logs. Reconcile with finance when monthly invoices arrive.
Good sign
The platform's share of spend rises week on week.
Warning sign
Direct spend grows faster than platform spend.
What to do: Find which kind of non-adoption holds back the three largest direct spenders.
03

How long does a new team take to reach its first production call?

Every week of waiting makes a direct call look easier.

How to measure it
For each new team, record the days from its access request to its first successful production call, a measure API providers call time to first call [8]. Also record the days from a new-model request to the model's arrival on the platform.
Good sign
Both are counted in days and falling.
Warning sign
Either runs to weeks, or a model review starts later than the service promise.
What to do: Find the slowest step for the last three teams and remove or automate it before adding any feature.

Numbers that look good but mislead

  • Teams onboarded and API keys issued cost a team nothing, so they rise while production use stands still.
  • Adoption as a share of all teams counts teams with too little traffic or risk to need the platform, so you chase the wrong teams.

Real examples

NamedBank's own blog and on-record interview

JPMorganChase

The bank launched its internal LLM Suite in summer 2024, reaching 200,000 onboarded users in eight months [9]. The bank's Derek Waldron said nobody was mandated to use it, and that phased onboarding created "a little bit of a fear of missing out". By October 2025, "a little under half of JPMorgan employees use gen AI tools every single day" [10].

The lessonOpen the platform one group at a time and let waiting groups see who has it, so demand builds before their turn.

NamedFirst-hand engineering blog

Uber

Uber built its GenAI Gateway after more than 60 use cases had grown separate ways of calling models. As described in July 2024, it copies the OpenAI API's interface, so teams keep their code libraries, and adds personal-data masking and audit logs [11].

The lessonMake your gateway accept the same calls as the provider API teams already use, so joining is a change of address, not a rewrite.

NamedSelf-reported, via trade press

Spotify

Spotify reports 99% voluntary internal adoption of Backstage, its developer portal, after about a year [12]. By Spotify's own estimate, other organisations using Backstage average about 10% adoption, and Spotify had to cut setup from 70 steps to 3–5 [13].

The lessonBefore building on a platform another company made popular, ask its adoption rate elsewhere and how many steps a new team takes to start, because that is what you are buying.
