The parser worked in the demo and fails on our real documents

Before the pilot is judged against the demo, decide which documents you promise to read, captured how, and who checks the rest.

Customer here means the team relying on the output: a lender or insurer buying your product, your IT services client, or your own operations team. Internal teams can read contract as service agreement, and sponsor as steering committee.

The challenge

What is happening. Your tool reads loan files for a mid-sized lender's credit operations team. In the demo, a cloud parser read 20 clean bank-generated PDFs almost perfectly. The pilot brought WhatsApp-forwarded statement photos, a 14-page statement with a table spanning pages, rent agreements with a seal over the tenant's name, and handwritten Marathi forms.

Why it hurts. Operations staff now re-check every field, so your tool adds a step instead of removing one. On one statement the parser quietly corrected a total that did not add up, hiding a sign of tampering. The review is in 5 weeks and nobody has defined success, so the sponsor will judge the pilot against the demo's 98%.

What is really going on

Operations staff describe three separate problems, and a better parser fixes only part of each.

01

The demo files were not the real mix

They say"It cannot read the scans or the phone photos."

What is really happening
Capture quality matters more than the parser. On an Allen Institute for AI benchmark, the best of nine systems scored 83 out of 100 overall but 50 on old scans; the weakest scored 29 [1].
How to spot it
Sort a week of files by how they arrived. If capture is the problem, errors cluster in phone photos and forwarded files.
What to do
Stop quoting one accuracy figure and promise each document group separately: one document type at one capture quality, such as phone photos of bank statements.
02

The parser is wrong with confidence

They say"The numbers looked fine, but the total was wrong."

What is really happening
Older OCR (software that turns page images into text) left blanks when it failed; AI parsers fill in believable values. One benchmark author reported models often fixing a printed total that did not match its line items [2].
How to spot it
Nothing is blank or garbled, so reviewers find these errors only by recalculating.
What to do
Recalculate totals from the line items on a plain OCR copy, because a model that corrected the total passes a check on its own output. Fields feeding credit or fraud decisions skip review only when such a check confirms them.
03

Handwriting and script are separate problems

They say"Typed English forms are fine. The handwritten and Marathi ones fail."

What is really happening
A June 2026 single-author preprint found systems close on clean Devanagari text (used for Marathi and Hindi) but up to 76 points apart out of 100 on real scans [3]. No independent benchmark covers Indian business documents yet, so your own test is the only evidence.
How to spot it
Split the error rate by script and by typed or handwritten, because the typed English rate hides the rest.
What to do
Treat each script, and handwriting, as a separate document group tested on real scans before you promise it.
Stop asking

How accurate is the parser?

Start asking

Which documents, captured how, can pass without a person, and how would we know if they went wrong?

The mistake most teams make

Running a parser contest on the customer's sample folder, then promising one accuracy figure for everything.

The sample folder holds files someone chose to show, so the contest picks the best engine for documents that were never the problem. The first phone photos break the single figure, staff stop trusting the output, and the savings vanish even on files the tool reads well. Run any contest on the worst real files instead.

The options you have

Most teams combine two or three, starting with those that shrink the problem before paying to solve it.

Promise only the documents that already work

Launch on the document groups that pass your tests today, send the rest to the customer's manual process, and add groups as they pass.

Best when
A few document types carry most of the volume.
What it costs you
The launch covers less than the demo implied, which the sponsor must accept and explain.
Watch out
It fails if the pain sits in the rejected files, such as phone photos from rural branches.

Get the data without reading the document

Where the data already exists as structured records, fetch them and keep the parser for everything else.

Best when
Your customers already use such channels, or can ask their applicants to.
What it costs you
Integration and consent flows take engineering time, and every extra step can lose applicants.
Watch out
It misses files outside these channels, such as small suppliers' paper invoices, which are the parser's real job.

Indian borrowers can share bank records with lenders through the Account Aggregator network, carrying 295 million+ transfers a month as of June 2026 [4]. RBI's KYC directions for commercial banks accept issuer-signed DigiLocker documents once the bank verifies the signature, so those need no reading [5]; NBFCs have separate directions [6]. Suppliers above ₹5 crore turnover have issued e-invoices since August 2023 [7], so only smaller suppliers' invoices need reading.

Fix the file where it is captured

Build checks into the upload screen so a blurred or glare-covered photo is retaken before the applicant or agent leaves, and mask Aadhaar and other national ID numbers before any parser or outside AI service sees them.

Best when
Your product or the customer's field staff control the upload, as in a lending app.
What it costs you
Uploading takes extra steps, and the capture screen needs its own design work.
Watch out
It fails for files forwarded by email or chat, with nobody there to retake them.

The same RBI directions require Aadhaar numbers to be redacted where authentication is not needed [5]. DPDP security duties apply from 14 May 2027 [8], with penalties up to ₹250 crore [9], and logs must be kept for at least a year [8], so an unmasked number in a log stays there.

Send each document type to the engine that reads it best

Use low-cost OCR for system-generated PDFs, a specialist vendor for bank statements and an AI model for handwriting, choosing each by how often it gives confident wrong values on your own files.

Best when
Several varied document types each carry real volume.
What it costs you
Every outside engine that sees customer documents needs infosec, data-residency and third-party risk approval, which can take months in a bank, so start before the test.
Watch out
It fails when the first step misreads a document's type, sending the page to the wrong engine and carrying the error through every later step [10]. Test the routing on your worst files as well as the engines.

Make human review part of the product

Design the product so a reviewer confirms only uncertain fields. In an August 2026 study, routing by the best model's confidence caught 2.43 times as many errors as random checks of the same 30% of fields [11].

Best when
Documents vary widely and one wrong field is expensive, as in credit, insurance claims or KYC.
What it costs you
Reviewer time stays in the running cost, so the business case should promise time saved per file, not fewer staff.
Watch out
It fails if you route on confidence scores untested on your own files; weaker models in the same study were far less reliable [11].

PM tools to put in place

A document mix grid

Rows are document types; columns are how they arrive: system-generated PDF, office scan, phone photo or forwarded file, with script split out where it matters. Each box shows its share of volume and one decision: fetch the data elsewhere, pass without review, send to a reviewer, or ask for a retake. Attach it to the contract with an accuracy target and audit method for each box's critical fields, the few that drive the decision. Operations, credit risk and compliance sign it off.

A set of the worst real files

Ask operations staff, not the sponsor, for the 50 files they struggle with most, and agree the correct value of every critical field. Keep them whole, because a May 2026 study found strong single-page scores did not reliably carry over to multi-page documents [12]. Fix the set for the pilot; at the review, a document group passes only if it clears this set and the weekly audit.

A review screen that shows where each value came from

Each field shows whether it passed automatically or a person confirmed it, and one click shows its source on the page. When a balance or total does not add up, the screen flags possible tampering instead of correcting it. Later users, such as credit analysts, see the same statuses.

Your first three steps

  1. 01

    Sort the last two weeks of real pilot files, not the sample folder, into the grid, and raise any data-access request today. You will see where volume and errors sit before anyone argues about engines.

  2. 02

    Run the current parser on the operations lead's 50 worst files and mark each error as blank or confidently wrong. The confident errors show which fields need an independent check before the review.

  3. 03

    Book 30 minutes with the sponsor, a credit risk lead and, in a regulated firm, your model risk contact to agree the critical fields (such as monthly income), the error limit on each, and the evidence validation will need.

What to measure, and how

Check these weekly, in this order.

01

Are operations staff relying on the output instead of re-checking it?

Accuracy saves nothing while staff re-check every field.

How to measure it
For each document group, sample reviewer minutes per file and count how many automatically passed fields staff edit anyway.
Good sign
Minutes per file fall, and edits to passed fields are rare.
Warning sign
Minutes stay flat, or staff keep editing fields the tool passed.
What to do: Sit with two reviewers for an afternoon to learn which fields they distrust and why, then change what the screen shows or return those documents to full review.
02

Are wrong values reaching decisions without anyone noticing?

A confident wrong value does more damage than a blank, and nobody sees it unless they look.

How to measure it
Each week, have someone re-type the critical fields of a random sample of automatically passed files without seeing the tool's values. Size the sample to expect about 10 errors at the agreed limit (about 5,000 fields at 0.2%), pooling weeks if volume is low.
Good sign
Audited errors stay within the limit while the share passed without review holds or rises.
Warning sign
Errors exceed the limit in any document group.
What to do: Return that group to full review the same day, and fix the cause before it passes unreviewed again.
03

Are reviewers still catching errors?

Reviewers who see correct fields all day start approving without reading, so errors go straight through.

How to measure it
With compliance approval, seed about 1% of the queue with test copies of real files, each with one wrong value, and count how many reviewers catch. Tag the copies so a missed one never reaches a real decision.
Good sign
Reviewers catch nearly all test values, across shifts and on busy days.
Warning sign
The catch rate falls, especially late in a shift or when volume rises.
What to do: Cut the queue per reviewer or rotate the work, and check the screen still makes uncertain fields stand out.

Numbers that look good but mislead

  • The straight-through processing rate (files passed with no human touch) when nobody audits them, which can rise along with wrong values.
  • One average accuracy figure, which blends easy and hard files, so a rise often means only an easier mix.

Real examples

NamedFirst-hand account

Alan

The French health insurer reached 70% automation on insurance documents with an AI pipeline, then found the model inventing values not on the page. It had learned from records staff corrected with facts not in the document; as Alan put it, "the examples taught it that those values should be there" [10].

The lessonHave reviewers record what the page says separately from what is true, and train the parser only on what the page says.

NamedVendor blog

Hyperscience

The document-processing vendor reported that only 11% of one competitor's straight-through documents were more than 98% correct [13].

The lessonAsk every shortlisted vendor what share of its straight-through documents were fully correct on your files.

NamedVendor product page

Perfios

The Indian bank-statement analyser says it supports 4,145 PDF statement formats [14].

The lessonCount the formats a document type arrives in before building a parser; when they run into thousands, buy from a specialist and spend your effort on types nobody sells.
